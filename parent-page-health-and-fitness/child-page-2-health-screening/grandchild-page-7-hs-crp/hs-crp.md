hs-CRP is a measure of low-level inflammation in the body. It's tested alongside cholesterol because ongoing inflammation also plays a part in heart disease. Mine improved between April and October 2026, but it's still not under my target of 1. My October blood draw came at a messy time, with a competition, a fresh elbow injury and a neck injury all in the weeks before, which makes this number harder to read.

## What it is

CRP (C-reactive protein) is made by the liver when there's inflammation anywhere in the body. During an infection or after an injury, it can jump into the tens or hundreds.

The **high-sensitivity** (hs) version measures very low levels, the 0–5 mg/L zone. At those levels it isn't picking up an infection; it's picking up background, low-grade inflammation. Persistent low-grade inflammation is linked with plaque build-up in the arteries, which is why the lab reports it as a heart-disease risk factor:

- **Under 1.0 mg/L:** low risk
- **1.0–3.0:** average risk
- **Over 3.0:** increased risk
- **Over 5.0:** suggests an infection or another source of inflammation, so the result can't be read as a heart-risk number

## What I'm aiming for

Under **1.0 mg/L**, the low-risk band. A lean, fit 34-year-old with good lipids would normally sit there.

The catch is that hs-CRP is very sensitive to what's going on in your body *right now*: a cold, a hard training week, a muscle strain or an injury can all push it up for days to weeks. One reading isn't a verdict. Two readings at least a couple of weeks apart, taken when you're well and not injured, are more meaningful.

## Why April was a problem

At 1.82 mg/L, April was in the "average risk" band. Taken alone that's not alarming, but April also had LDL above its limit, ApoB above target and more fat around my trunk. A higher hs-CRP alongside those is a slightly worse combination than any one of them alone.

I don't have notes on what was happening with training or illness just before the April test, so I can't say how much of the 1.82 was background inflammation and how much was temporary.

## Why October is better (but not there yet)

hs-CRP dropped to **1.24 mg/L**. What probably helped:

- **Much less body fat.** Fat tissue, especially visceral fat around the organs, releases inflammatory signals. My body fat went from 19.3% to 9.55% and visceral fat almost halved.
- **Better lipids** and **more training** overall.

So why isn't it under 1? There are a few strong candidates around the October draw, and I can't separate them:

- **Competition training.** In the weeks before my BJJ competition on 11 October, I was training 6 times a week. Hard training causes muscle damage and short-term inflammation.
- **My elbow injury.** I re-injured my right elbow (an armbar hyperextension) at the competition on 11 October, **9 days before** the blood test. A fresh soft-tissue injury can easily keep CRP up for a while.
- **My neck.** I was diagnosed with cervical disc herniations in early October, and still had symptoms around the draw.
- **The weight cut** and post-competition recovery put extra stress on the body.

There's also one factor pushing the other way: I was on etoricoxib, an anti-inflammatory, from about 2 to 12 October for my neck. It had stopped about a week before the draw, so its effect had probably worn off, but I can't rule it out completely. (Etoricoxib is covered properly in the [ALT article](/health-and-fitness/health-screening/alt-and-etoricoxib).)

Given all that, 1.24 is probably *higher* than my true baseline. But until it's retested at a calmer time, that's a guess.

## What's next

- Retest hs-CRP when I'm recovered: elbow and neck settled, no competition prep, no hard sessions in the 48–72 hours before, and no recent cold.
- If it's still over 1 then, it's worth a conversation with my doctor about other sources of inflammation.
